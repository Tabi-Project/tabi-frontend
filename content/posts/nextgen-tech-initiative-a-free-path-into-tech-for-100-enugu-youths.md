For many young people in Enugu, the gap between "can use a computer" and "can get hired in tech" is wider than it should be. It's not a talent gap. It's a training gap: few structured, practical programmes exist to turn basic digital literacy into skills that actually open doors to income.

The NextGen Tech Initiative is designed to close that gap. It's a 12-week tech training programme created by Tabi Academy (operated under the Tabi Empowerment & Educational Foundation, "TEE Foundation") in partnership with Emerging Elite's Foundation, built to equip 100 Enugu youths with practical, job-ready tech skills at no cost to participants.

## **What it is**

Starting August 22, 2026, the programme runs every Saturday f for 12 weeks, combining in-person classroom sessions and virtual classes with short self-paced videos participants watch ahead of each class. In-person time is reserved for hands-on practice, not lectures.

It's completely free and open to anyone who can operate a computer and open basic applications (no prior coding or AI experience needed). Applications are open to everyone, but the programme is designed first and foremost for youths based in Enugu.

## What participants will learn

Over 12 weeks, participants move from foundational digital skills to genuinely marketable ones:

1. Google Workspace (Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive, Forms) for real workplace tasks
2. Advanced Excel and dashboards (formulas, pivot tables, data visualization)
3. Automation with Google App Scripts
4. Professional digital communication (email etiquette, LinkedIn, video calls)
5. AI fluency and prompting (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, etc.) as a practical work and learning tool
6. Web development (HTML, CSS, JavaScript), ending with a live, published website

The programme closes with a capstone project, a Demo Day presentation in front of facilitators and community guests, and a certificate for participants who meet the attendance and deliverable requirements.

## Why this matters beyond the 12 weeks

The immediate goal is straightforward: give 100 Enugu youths practical tech skills and a real shot at employability, whether that means a job, freelance tech work, or the confidence to build something of their own.

The longer-term goal is bigger than one cohort. Participants who go through this programme, and go on to build careers or businesses from it, are meant to eventually return as trainers and facilitators for the cohorts that follow. Over time, that's meant to compound: more Enugu youths economically empowered through tech jobs and entrepreneurship, more of them equipped to train the next group, and a gradual shift in how much opportunity is actually available to young people in Enugu, without needing to leave the city to find it. It's a decade-long bet on building that capacity locally, one cohort at a time.
