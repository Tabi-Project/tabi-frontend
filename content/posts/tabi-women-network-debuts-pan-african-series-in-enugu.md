# Tabi Women Network Debuts Pan‑African Series in Enugu

## Executive Summary

On April 4th, 2026, the **Tabi Women Network** hosted an intimate, invite‑only lunch for eight high‑level women leaders across Tech, Business, and Governance in Enugu. Moving away from traditional conference formats, the three‑hour “cozy engagement” used structured **“Cue Cards”** to skip small talk and address the real challenges of African leadership, policy, and cross‑sector collaboration. The event successfully identified immediate partnership opportunities and onboarded attendees into the Tabi pan‑African directory.

---

## About Tabi & the Women’s Network

**Tabi** (under the Tabi Empowerment & Educational Foundation) makes practical technology education and resources accessible to women in tech and business.

The **Tabi Women Network** is the pan‑African network arm of this vision. Its goal is to build an accessible, living directory of women making an impact across Africa. By hosting monthly lunches across multiple cities and countries, Tabi is creating a curated map of influence that allows women to find the right partners, exchange information, and bridge the gap between governance and private industry.

---

## The Women at the Table

The cohort consisted of eight multi‑hyphenate leaders:

- **Sophia Ahuoyiza** – Software Engineer, Product Manager, Co‑founder of Yelobyte Studios, and Executive Director of Tabi Empowerment & Educational Foundation.
- **Lady Benedeth Maduka** – Retired Senior Police Officer and Executive Director of the Benedeth Maduka Foundation (GBV Specialist).
- **Barrister Sylvia Agbana** – Chairperson, League of Women Voters Nigeria (Enugu); Former Chairperson, FIDA Enugu.
- **Augusta Nneka Nnadi** – Special Assistant on Strategy & Communications (SEDC); Politics and Policy Specialist.
- **Betty Agbo** – Filmmaker, Actor, and Creative Director at Betscents.
- **Amarachi Okeke** – Product Manager and Co‑runner of Sustainable Initiatives NGO.
- **Tracy Jerry Ugwu** – Co‑founder of an Inclusive Nursery & Primary School (Special Needs) and Product Marketing Lead.
- **Ijeoma Achu** – Programmes Manager, Tabi.

## The Flow of the Afternoon

The event kicked off at **2:00 PM prompt**, establishing Tabi’s culture of good time management. Following introductions and a welcome speech by Sophia, a video message from Tabi’s Co‑director, **Ihuoma Agbaru**, was shared to reiterate the value of intentional gathering. The core of the afternoon was the **Roundtable Session**, driven by interactive Cue Cards.

---

## The Conversations: Roundtable Cue Cards

### On Career Decisions and Impact

> **Question:** *What’s one decision you made in your career or business that changed everything — and would you make it again?*

**Lady Benedeth** highlighted that *impact has no retirement age*. Since retiring from the Police Force, she has focused on GBV advocacy and learning new languages (Spanish and French). Her contribution reminded the table that a woman’s life can continue to expand even as she gets older.

### On the Hardest Part of Operating in Africa

> **Question:** *What is the hardest thing about operating in your industry or sector in Africa — and what keeps you going anyway?*

The group centered on **Consistency**. The drive to look back and realize they impacted themselves, the community, and other women is what keeps these leaders showing up in difficult environments.

### On Fear and Taking Action

> **Question:** *What is one thing you wish someone had told you earlier in your career?*

**Betty Agbo** shared personal insights on the fear of taking steps. The group discussed the importance of not being afraid to take bold actions and having mentors who nudge you to move sooner.

### On Leadership and Intuition

> **Question:** *What does leadership mean to you, and who taught you that?*

**Lady Benedeth** advocated for *empathy over authority*, while **Amarachi** and **Tracy** noted that being firm doesn’t require being “loud” or “mean.” **Sophia** added that women often doubt their abilities and say “no” to themselves before the world does. She advised that intuition is a muscle built through trial and error, and leaders must trust it.

### On Politics and Governance

> **Question:** *What is one myth about your industry, role, or sector that you would like to put to rest?*

This triggered a robust debate. **Augusta Nneka Nnadi** argued against *tokenism*, pushing instead for qualified women with “proof of work.” **Barr. Sylvia** discussed the benefits of tokenism while agreeing on the points Augusta raised. The group talked about how societal limitations (late meetings, marriage, family) often restrict women in politics. **Sophia** concluded that we must refine systems to account for the uniqueness of women’s lives, making the workforce sustainable for mothers.

### On Success and Legacy

The table agreed that success is deeply personal and ever‑changing. Everyone mentioned their **mothers** as the first woman who taught them to lead. We realized we were all inspired by women who lived authentically, took up room, and defied the odds.

## The “Long Game” & the Luminary Project

Before transitioning to the final networking session, **Sophia** delivered a concluding charge on the vital importance of authenticity and self‑trust. She nudged the women to leave the room with the strong belief that they can achieve all they set their hearts to, but reminded them that they must not be afraid to simply **be**.

Central to her speech was the concept of the **“Long Game.”** Sophia urged the women to prioritise rest and engage in activities that enrich the mind, body, and soul. To lead effectively and sustain impact over decades, one needs to be healthy and take care of the body.

As part of this long‑term vision, Sophia introduced the **Luminary Project** – an open‑source initiative born from Tabi’s partnership with **Rise Academy by Risevest**. Luminary is designed to spotlight and profile women making an impact in their fields, and **every woman who engages with the Tabi Women Network will be profiled in this directory**.

This project is one of three key products emerging from the partnership, which also includes:

- **Bloom After** – A digital resource hub for women facing Postpartum Depression (PPD).  
- **Ekehi** – A financial resource hub tailored for women in business and founders.
