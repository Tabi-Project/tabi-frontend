Right now, in this first week, or say, second week, everything probably feels a little bigger than usual. New faces. A new WhatsApp group already buzzing with messages. A program walkthrough that mentioned words like “capstone” and “App Script” — things you’ve maybe never touched before or terms you might have never heard before.  If you’re feeling a mix of excitement and “wait, can I actually do this?” — good. That feeling means you’re paying attention. It means this matters to you.

That’s exactly why this first goal-setting exercise isn’t just admin. It’s the one thing standing between “I hope I get better at computers” and actually knowing, in twelve weeks, that you did.

Why “I Want to Get Better at Tech” Isn’t a Goal!

Here’s the truth: almost everyone walks into a programme like this with a version of the same wish — I want to be good with computers. I want to get a job. I want to stop feeling behind. Everyone is making money in tech. 

Those are real feelings. But they’re not goals you can act on tomorrow morning. A wish like that has no shape. You can’t tell if you’re closer to it in Week 6 than you were in Week 1, because there’s nothing to measure it against. And when you can’t see progress, it’s easy to feel like you’re not making any — even when you are.

This is where SMART goals come in. Not as a corporate worksheet exercise, but as a way of turning that big, fuzzy hope into something you can actually hold onto on the hard days — and there will be a few of those between now and Week 12.

What SMART Actually Means (Without the Jargon)

Specific — Say exactly what you want. Not “get better at Excel,” but “be able to build a working dashboard with pivot tables and two charts.”

Measurable — Attach a number, a deliverable, or a clear yes/no to it. Something you can point to and say, “there — that’s done.”

Achievable — Stretch yourself, but don’t set a goal that only makes sense if everything goes perfectly. Twelve weeks is real time, but it’s not infinite time.

Relevant — Make sure the goal actually matters to you — not just something that sounds impressive to say out loud.

Time-bound — Attach it to this programme’s actual calendar. Week 4. Week 8. Week 12. Real dates change how seriously you take a goal.

Turning It Personal

Let’s say your honest, in-your-head goal right now is: “I want to be able to get a remote job using these skills.”

That’s a beautiful reason to be here. It’s also too big to act on this Saturday. So we shrink it — not because the big goal doesn’t matter, but because the big goal is built from twelve smaller, doable ones.

It might become something like:; “By Week 4, I want to have a Google Drive that’s fully organised, a formatted document, and a 5-slide deck I’d be comfortable sharing with a stranger. By Week 7-8, I want a working App Script automation and a LinkedIn profile that actually represents me. By Week 12, I want to walk into Demo Day with a live website I built myself, and present it without my hands shaking.”  \
\
Notice what happened there. The big dream didn’t disappear. It just got translated into checkpoints you can actually see yourself hitting — checkpoints that live inside weeks you’re already going to show up for anyway.

The Goal Card Isn’t Just Paper! 

When you fill out your goal card this week, resist the urge to write what sounds good. Write what’s true. If your honest goal is “I want to stop feeling embarrassed when someone mentions a spreadsheet,” write that — then translate it into something SMART. There’s no wrong starting point, only starting points that are dressed up to impress someone instead of built to help you.

Keep that card somewhere you’ll actually see it — not buried in a folder you’ll open again in Week 12. Because the version of you sitting here in Week 1, a little unsure, a little hopeful, is the only person who knows exactly why you signed up. Twelve weeks from now, on Demo Day, you’ll want to be able to look back at what you wrote and see how far the distance between those two people really was.

That distance is the whole point of the programme. The goal card is just how you make sure you notice it.
